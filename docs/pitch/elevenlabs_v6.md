## Script

**V01 — Cold open (≈ 14 s)**
Around sixty CE, a Greek physician wrote down what every plant could heal.
Sixteen centuries later, an English herbalist wrote over him.
The older layer still shows through.

**V02 — Title (≈ 4 s)**
This is Palimpsest.

**V03 — The question (≈ 15 s)**
Herbals are mined for drug leads all the time — but by hand, in translation, without controls.
We asked two questions. What survived sixteen centuries of copying?
And has modern science actually looked?

**V04 — Reading the Greek (≈ 18 s)**
Palimpsest reads Dioscorides in the original Greek — all eight hundred and twenty-two chapters — and Culpeper's Complete Herbal, from sixteen fifty-three.
Every ancient name becomes a modern species. Every remedy becomes a medical concept. And every claim keeps its exact words.

**V05 — Can you trust it? (≈ 17 s)**
Can you trust a machine reading ancient Greek?
Against a blind reference, extraction scores an F1 of zero point eight six in Greek.
And against experts' own plant identifications, we match the expert species seventy percent of the time — and the genus ninety-one.

**V06 — Result one (≈ 17 s)**
First answer. Culpeper repeats Dioscorides two point six five times more often than chance.
Still twice as often when we match plants by how much was written about them — and one point six times, even within the same botanical family.

**V07 — Result two (≈ 18 s)**
Second answer. Remedies the herbals agree on are far more likely to have been studied: forty-five percent, against thirty-two percent for other uses of the same plants.
But once studied, they are not studied more.
Modern science has looked — but not looked hard.

**V08 — Robustness (≈ 9 s)**
We searched again under every synonym of every plant. The conclusions held.

**V09 — The leads (≈ 16 s)**
That gap becomes sixty research leads — persistent, plausible, and barely studied.
Rue for the eyes ranks first. Every lead shows its sources, its chemistry, and its warnings.
These are hypotheses — not medical advice.

**V10 — The product (≈ 17 s)**
Everything is open. Scrape the page and watch sixteen centuries peel away.
Read the Greek and the English side by side, trace any claim to the papers behind it — or ask an AI assistant, through our open MCP server.

**V11 — Who it is for (≈ 12 s)**
For historians, a measure of how knowledge travels.
For drug discovery, an explainable shortlist.
For medicine, the evidence — and its limits — in one place.

**V12 — End (≈ 6 s)**
Palimpsest.
The older layer still shows through.
